What the mouth can say

Phonon (the voice these apps speak with) is a text-to-speech model trained on written prose. It has no sound-spelling stage: a written hum like "mm-hm" is a foreign word to it and gets sounded out. This page is the audition table for the reaction vocabulary. Each line is the same voice speaking the written text on the left. Run scripts/phonon-audition.sh to render the audio next to this page.

Review of 2026-10-26. Every candidate pronounces correctly, including the letter-string hums. One problem: vowel sounds ("Um.", "Ah.") play over the top — too loud, too emphatic. The in-sentence examples show it well.

Onset measurements (2026-10-26). The ear was right, and no spelling fixes it: "Um", "uhm", "ehm", "umm" — lower case or not — all reach full energy within 5 ms of onset, like an exclamation. A human "um" swells in over 150–300 ms. What renders soft naturally: murmurs ("Mhm." swells over 145 ms), soft leads ("hmm, let me check." — 45 ms), and vowels tucked behind a soft first word ("So, um, one moment." — 125 ms). So a vowel never starts an utterance; if "ums and ahs" are wanted at the start, the mouth needs an attack fade (about 120 ms) on phatic playback — a mouth-side change, decided separately.

Meanwhile the shipping vocabulary stays words ("Yeah.", "Right.", "I see.", "Got it.", "Okay.", "Sure."). Also known: the text normalizer folds ellipses away ("Um..." speaks as "Um."), so softness has to come from wording, not dots.

Voice sweep (2026-10-27: the voice is the expressivity knob)

Phonon has no emotes (verified: no special tokens, prose in and out) — but the voice carries the delivery. Same text, same model, different voice:

The apps speak through the Core ML build of these same weights, so what you hear here is what callers hear. To render the set: build the ptts CLI (cargo build --release --bin ptts --features cli in an xn-ptts checkout), then run scripts/phonon-audition.sh. It renders the wav files and copies this page next to them (audio is regenerable and stays out of git). The reaction vocabulary policy lives in BackchannelFiller/TurnFiller (the hum fold).
